How many ways out?

Type your address. See the fire zone you are in, the dead-end streets around you, and how far the nearest fire station is.

Street name on its own works too.
This is not an evacuation plan and it will never tell you that you are safe. In an emergency follow the Fire Department, not this page.

What you can do about it

Book a defensible space inspection The Fire Department inspects hill properties every year, March to November. Free. Get help clearing vegetation Free City services including green waste removal and chipping. Claim the home hardening rebate A tax rebate for ember-resistant work on your house. Set up for evacuation Evacuation zones, alerts, and what to do when a warning comes.

The single most useful thing on this page is the dead-end count. If the streets around you terminate, everyone leaves the same way you do. Knowing that now is worth more than knowing it during an evacuation.

Where this comes from

Every number on this page is computed from map layers the City of Berkeley publishes openly. Nothing here is guesswork, and nothing here is official.

Berkeley Fire Department The official source for everything on this page. If anything here disagrees with them, they are right. berkeleyfiresafe.org The City's own address lookup, defensible space rules, and the wildfire resilience dashboard.
  • Fire zones and hazards Hill Fire Zones, CalFire Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone, landslide and Alquist-Priolo fault zones, from the City's ArcGIS server.
  • Streets and single-exit areas City street centrelines for the whole region, not just Berkeley. An area is a part of the network that reaches everywhere else through one junction.
  • Buildings The City's building footprint layer, fetched for your neighbourhood when you search. Your own building is matched by parcel number.
  • Addresses The City's 62,090 published address points.
  • The studies behind it Access Impaired Neighborhood Analysis (SB 99) and Evacuation Route Safety, Capacity, and Viability Analysis (AB 747), both by KLD Engineering for the City of Berkeley, early 2024. Those reports are the real record; this page just makes the same layers searchable.

Before you rely on any of this

It is not official. This site is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to the City of Berkeley, the Berkeley Fire Department, or any government agency. It was built by a Berkeley resident using data those agencies publish openly. Where it disagrees with them, they are right and this is wrong.
Never use it in an emergency. Call 911. Follow the Fire Department and the County's emergency alerts. Nothing here is an evacuation plan, an evacuation route, or a judgment that anywhere is safe. It is a way to read published maps before anything happens, and that is all.
It is probably wrong in places. Single-exit areas are worked out from street centrelines and have not been checked on the ground. Where the City's data has a hole, a through street can look like it has one exit, and where a private drive or a gate connects, an area may have a second way out the map cannot see. Distances are straight lines, not driving distances. Map layers are a snapshot and the City updates them on its own schedule.
